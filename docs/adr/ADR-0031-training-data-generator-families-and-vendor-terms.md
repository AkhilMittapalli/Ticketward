# ADR-0031: Training-data generator families and vendor-terms compliance

## Context and Problem Statement

Ticketward trains and evaluates on synthetic tickets (S-12). v1.0 named Anthropic Claude as the likely Family A
generator for train/val. The ERPROT synthetic-data research found that Anthropic's Usage Policy (effective
2025-09-15) lists "Utilization of inputs and outputs to train an AI model" as prohibited platform abuse unless
Anthropic has given prior authorization. The prohibition covers every AI model, including a small classifier, so a
Claude-generated training set is not permitted as written (change record A-01; R-10 materialized).

The evaluation design also needs:
* a **different model family** for test_synth than for train/val, to avoid generator-style leakage (ADR-0016);
* a test generator that is **not Claude**, because E6 (Claude triaging as the frontier reference) would otherwise get
  a home-field advantage, and Claude-proposed labels would bias gold toward Claude's conventions;
* licences that do not make the fine-tuned SLM a "derivative" with naming or use restrictions.

The generation budget is $15, including pilots and regeneration.

Which models generate train/val and test data, and how is the no-training-on-Claude rule enforced over the project's
life (training data, feedback exports, a future draft LoRA, judge outputs)?

## Decision Drivers

* **Vendor-terms compliance:** no model's terms may forbid training on its outputs, or impose derivative/naming
  obligations on the SLM.
* **Evaluation validity:** different families for train and test; test not generated by the E6 frontier; the
  generator never labels test data.
* Quality and diversity of generated tickets at low cost ($15).
* Reproducibility and provenance: every record says which model, endpoint, prompt and terms snapshot produced it.
* Enforcement: a CI check, not a convention.

## Considered Options

1. Family A `openai/gpt-oss-120b` (train/val), Family B `mistralai/Mistral-Large-3-675B-Instruct-2512` (test_synth),
   never Claude for any training data, CI provenance check on every training export (chosen)
2. Claude as Family A (the v1.0 default)
3. Llama or Gemma as Family A
4. The same family for train and test

## Decision Outcome

Chosen option: option 1, because both models are Apache-2.0 open-weights releases whose terms (as snapshotted) permit
training on outputs. They come from two unrelated families, and neither is the E6 frontier or a likely base SLM.

**Family A (train/val):**
* `openai/gpt-oss-120b` (Apache-2.0; its usage policy asks only for safe use and compliance with law);
* prompt family P-A: persona-first, zero-shot; templates `pa.t1–t4` for train and `pa.t5–t6` for val;
* reasoning effort low, one ticket per call;
* returns ticket + **proposed** labels + a `self_check` note;
* access through a hosted API whose terms were verified, or self-hosted vLLM;
* fallback: `Qwen/Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507` or DeepSeek (licences to verify before use).

**Family B (test_synth):**
* `mistralai/Mistral-Large-3-675B-Instruct-2512` (Apache-2.0; API id `mistral-large-2512`, verify on Mistral's model
  list);
* prompt family P-B: scenario-first, two stages (a neutral case timeline, then the customer's message), with
  different templates, pools and framing;
* **gold labels come from the scenario spec plus 100% human review; the generator never labels**;
* fallback: `mistralai/Mistral-Small-4-119B-2603`. Family B is never Claude.

**Claude's role:** E6 (frontier-only reference), an optional LLM judge (reported separately, never replacing the human
audit), and the P7 frontier drafter. **Claude outputs never enter any training data.** That covers:
* train/val;
* NG-08 feedback exports (`GET /admin/feedback/export` excludes Anthropic-produced records);
* the optional G-3 draft LoRA data;
* judge outputs (which live under `evals/runs/`, never `data/`).

**Terms and provenance:**
* Terms are snapshotted with retrieval dates in `docs/legal/generator-terms-2026-09-26.md`, and re-verified at P1
  start and before any dataset is published. The gpt-oss host's terms and any separate Mistral usage policy are
  **verify at build**.
* Every record carries `generator_family` (`openai_gpt_oss | mistral | human | public_bitext`), `generator_model`,
  `generator_endpoint` (host + model id + request date, + HF revision if self-hosted), `prompt_family`,
  `prompt_version`, `template_id` and `terms_snapshot_id`.

**Budget:** $15, including a 50 + 50 pilot and regeneration. Estimates in config are dated. The binding cost is owner
review time (R-22).

**Amended 2026-09-27 (owner decision D-07): Family B is `deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V3.2` on DeepInfra.** DeepInfra, the Family A host, does not serve Mistral Large 3. The owner chose DeepSeek-V3.2 so that one account and one key serve both families.

- It is MIT-licensed, 685B parameters, and served in fp4 (recorded as `generator_quantization`).
- It costs $0.26 / $0.38 per 1M input/output tokens, about $1.8 for test_synth.
- It still satisfies every driver: a different family from Family A (gpt-oss) and from the base-SLM candidates (Qwen), never Claude, and outputs free to use.
- Mistral Large 3 on Mistral La Plateforme is kept as a config-only alternative (`generator_family: mistral` stays valid for test_synth). Evidence and page hashes: the D-07 addendum in `docs/legal/generator-terms-2026-09-27.md`.

### Consequences

* Good, because the training data is terms-compliant, and a single CI check guards every path by which Claude output
  could leak into training.
* Good, because cross-family test data and generator-independent gold labels make the headline numbers resistant to
  generator style and to E6 home-field bias.
* Good, because open-weights generators can be self-hosted if a host's terms change.
* Bad, because two new vendors or hosts must be term-checked, and their terms can change (R-16). Mitigations:
  snapshots, re-verification points, and fallback generators.
* Bad, because gpt-oss needs its "harmony" chat format and adds reasoning-token overhead. The pilot measures it.
* Neutral, because Claude may replace gpt-oss as Family A only with Anthropic's written authorization, recorded in an
  amendment of this ADR. Family B still must not be Claude.

### Confirmation

* CI job `data-provenance` (T-DATA-provenance, §14.5 step 7):
  * train/val records only `openai_gpt_oss` or `human`;
  * test_synth only `mistral`;
  * test_hard only `human` with `llm_assisted=false`;
  * test_ood only `public_bitext`;
  * no Anthropic `generator_family`/`provider` in any file under `data/` or in any training export (including
    feedback exports and the G-3 draft LoRA data).
* Feedback-export test: `GET /admin/feedback/export` omits every record produced by an Anthropic model, and the
  export is audited (`feedback.export`).
* `docs/legal/generator-terms-2026-09-26.md` exists, and every record's `terms_snapshot_id` resolves to it.
* Leakage C4 (structural disjointness of templates, scenario seeds, personas, companies) passes in CI (ADR-0016).
* Pilot report (50 P-A + 50 P-B): tokens, refusal yield, artifact and rule-check rates.

## Pros and Cons of the Options

### gpt-oss-120b (A) + Mistral Large 3 (B), never Claude for training (chosen)

* Good, because both are Apache-2.0 from unrelated families, the terms permit training use (as snapshotted), neither
  is the E6 model, and they are cheap.
* Bad, because host terms need verification, and gpt-oss has format and reasoning overhead.

### Claude as Family A

* Good, because the generation quality is high, and it was already the v1.0 plan.
* Bad, because Anthropic's Usage Policy forbids using outputs to train any AI model without authorization. This is a
  terms violation, not a trade-off.

### Llama or Gemma as Family A

* Good, because they are capable open-weights generators.
* Bad, because Llama's licence requires "Llama" at the start of the name of models trained on its outputs, plus
  attribution. Gemma's terms treat models trained on its synthetic outputs as "Model Derivatives" with pass-through
  use restrictions. Either would burden the SLM, which may also share a family with the bake-off winner (ADR-0011).

### The same family for train and test

* Good, because it is simplest and cheapest.
* Bad, because shared generator style inflates metrics (R-01), which defeats ADR-0016.
